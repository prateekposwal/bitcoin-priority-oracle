Bitcoin State Cost Research

Open research — the fee market prices congestion (inclusion). Does it price permanence (lifetime storage)?

Problem: The Fee Market Prices Congestion, Not Storage

Every inscription that enters a block adds data every full node must store forever. The miner who included it captured the fee — but the cost of storing that data for the next 20+ years is borne by every future node operator. The SegWit 4× witness discount (BIP-141) was designed for malleability, not state economics. It accidentally made data inscriptions viable at scale.

These proportions shift during inscription mania. The question: should data-heavy transactions face higher fees because they impose permanent storage costs on the network?

The Key Distinction: Congestion vs Permanence

Congestion pricing Permanence cost
What it prices Entry into the next block Lifetime storage in every node
Who pays Sender (once) All future node operators (forever)
Time horizon ~10 minutes (1 block) Indefinite
Handled by fee market? ✅ Yes ❌ Unpriced externality

The fee market handles congestion. The question this research has not settled: is the permanence externality real and significant? If most nodes run pruned and don't care about historical data, the fee market is sufficient.

Reference: CashTokens (Bitcoin Cash)

Bitcoin Cash's native token protocol (CHIP-2022-02) offers an interesting contrast. Tokens are consensus-enforced with 40-byte NFT commitments — no envelope hacks. BCH also has no SegWit, meaning no 4× witness discount subsidizing data. The result: inscription activity on BCH (0.5% of 32 MB blocks) causes zero congestion. This validates our SegWit discount analysis but doesn't help Bitcoin — CashTokens required a hard fork.

Exploratory Directions

After two failed attempts (v1: Priority Oracle, v2: Externality Fee), the community feedback pointed to relay policy as the only viable lever. Relay nodes are the only participants who both bear storage costs and have pricing power (they set relay policy). Below are three directions being explored.

UTXO-Aware Relay Fee Recommended

Extend Bitcoin Core's minrelaytxfee so relay nodes can apply a fee multiplier for transactions exceeding a state footprint threshold. Each node sets their own multiplier.
✅ Each node sets its own price — market mechanism, not a tax
✅ No consensus change — purely relay policy
✅ Miners unaffected — they mine whatever reaches them
⚠ Risk: low adoption by relay node operators

BIP for State-Conscious Relay Exploring

A BIP that standardizes what Direction A implements — defines state impact metrics, recommends fee schedules, and provides wallet-side fee estimation guidance.
✅ Creates ecosystem alignment without requiring anyone to adopt
✅ Formalizes existing practice (ordinal filtering nodes)
✅ BIP process ensures thorough incentive analysis
⚠ Scrutiny is high — but that's the point

Multi-Tier Relay Market Speculative

Relay nodes advertise tiered fee schedules during peer handshake. Wallets discover the cheapest relay path for their transaction type. A genuine market for relay services.
✅ Most market-driven approach
⚠ Requires P2P protocol changes
⚠ Higher complexity — only worth exploring if A/B prove insufficient

The Journey

v1
Priority Oracle
Classify tx as financial/data, allocate 30% floor. Failure: miners won't leave fees on the table. Voluntary classification with no economic consequence is a signaling game with zero equilibrium.
v2
Externality Fee
Price the "true storage cost" of data permanence via formula. Failure: any formula-based fee is an arbitrary tax, not a market price. No mechanism exists to discover the "true cost" of a transaction to the network.
v3
Relay Policy Research
Surveying existing work on state expiry, UTXO commitment, and block weight reform. Three exploratory directions above. No premature solutions — just open research. Full survey on GitHub.
