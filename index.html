Fee-to-Storage Ratio

Every transaction competes for 4 million weight units per block. The fee you pay gets you into the next one. But the data you leave behind lives in every full node forever—and nobody prices that cost.

Works Well

Fee Market

Bitcoin’s fee market is remarkably efficient at one thing: allocating scarce block space to the transactions that value it most. When mempools are full, the highest-paying transactions clear. When they’re empty, fees settle near zero. This is the congestion-pricing mechanism Satoshi described—and it works.

Unpriced

Permanence Cost

Every transaction that confirms imposes a storage cost on every full node that will ever sync that block. That cost compounds: one inscription written today must be stored, indexed, and served by thousands of nodes for the life of the network. The fee market ignores this entirely. We call it the permanence externality—and it grows with every block.
